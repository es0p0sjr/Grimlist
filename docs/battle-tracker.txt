Battle tracker: first scoring UI (2026-10-14)

Entry point
Open a saved army, then its menu > Battle tracker. The army must use Warhammer
40,000 11th edition and have an available Force Disposition selected to start
a new battle. Existing records remain accessible even if the roster changes.

Setup and presentation
- Choose opponent name/disposition, first player, and each player's Fixed or
  Tactical secondary mode. Fixed players choose two different eligible cards.
- Both primaries are assigned from the existing verified disposition matrix.
- Setup is locked on starting a battle; changing the roster later does not
  rewrite the army snapshot, recorded rules or primary assignments.
- Generated 10-character battle code identifies the battle. Serverless invitation
  and response files can establish the same battle on both devices; see
  docs/battle-exchange.txt. Scores remain independent. Compare is future work.
- Invitation generation/joining is separate from turn order. After exchanging
  armies, Open battle asks each player to select the agreed first player before
  Start battle. Existing battles resume without changing order or scores.
- Totals stay above the scroll area. Each player starts with 10 Battle Ready
  VP, editable separately. Going-first player is always above going-second.
- Each player has CP, primary conditions, a separator, then secondaries.
  Large separator between players. Previous/Next remain at the bottom.
- Each player's secondary score summary has a Show/Hide button. Collapsing
  hides that player's secondary cards, scoring and draw/discard controls while
  leaving the summary visible. The two sections are independent and keep their
  state across round changes during the current tracker visit. Expanding keeps
  pending picker selections; neither action changes or saves battle data.
- Android round swipes belong only to the fixed round heading, outside the
  scrolling area. Previous/Next buttons remain available.
- Condition changes update labels in place; structural changes preserve scroll.
  Changing rounds returns to the top. Round 5 leads to the scoreboard.

Scoring
All 25 primary and 18 secondary definitions are embedded for offline use.
Cards show applicable timing, conditions, values, local limits and expandable
instructions/actions. Players enter counts or mark conditions achieved; the app
does the arithmetic. It cannot infer battlefield positions or eligibility.
OR choices are exclusive. Cumulative conditions need the base condition;
subset bonuses cannot count more models/objectives than the base count.
Stored counts survive caps and earlier-round corrections.
Scores are recomputed chronologically (round, turn, Command phase/end of turn):
- Primary and secondary each have a separate 15 VP/player/battle-round cap.
- Each source has a 45 VP/player/battle cap.
- Fixed secondaries additionally have a 20 VP/card/player/battle cap.
- End-of-battle primary conditions are separate from round 5's 15 VP cap,
  while remaining inside the 45 VP primary cap.
- Tactical cards can score once, at one timing, and cannot score before drawn.
- CP records distinguish Core gains, extra gains (max 1/round) and spent CP.
  Spending cannot make any round's balance negative. Navigation grants nothing.
- Tactical cards offer Discard and Shuffle Back. Discard keeps the card out of
  future random/manual draws; Shuffle Back returns it to the draw pool. Both
  preserve manual CP counters and are available for unscored active cards,
  including carried cards. A discard can be followed by a replacement draw.
- See discard pool lists each player's discarded cards. Recover restores a card
  to play without spending a draw or changing CP. Discard/recovery history is
  retained, including repeated recoveries. Recovered cards cannot score in rounds
  before recovery. Fixed or already scored cards cannot be removed this way.
- Older saved discard/replacement CP totals remain supported, including after
  recovery. Their automatic CP summary only appears where old events exist.
- Draw random secondary selects from each player's remaining Tactical cards.
  Recorded cards cannot repeat; players have independent pools. Draw limits
  still apply. Defend Stronghold is left in the pool for later rounds because
  its first-round instruction requires shuffling it back. Manual draws remain.
- The scoreboard runs from Round 1 to Round 5. Each round has Primary and
  Secondary sections for both players, one capped VP total per scored card,
  discarded/shuffled/recovered card names, and concise CP spent/balance lines.
  No condition-by-condition explanations appear. Battle Ready and end-of-battle
  primary points remain separate; end-of-battle scoring controls are collapsed
  beneath the totals, available only for missions that use them.
- Finish battle is available during play and in history. Early finishing keeps
  recorded scores/CP and labels later rounds Not played. Reopen battle resumes
  the recorded final round. Finishing from an earlier view preserves later data.

Persistence
BattleStore saves one JSON file per battle under the platform data directory's
battles/ folder. Atomic temporary-file replacement and a serialized async writer
avoid partial writes and stale overlapping saves. Army and army-rule references
are snapshotted. Existing army/database records are not mutated. The lobby lists
all this army's local battles, including completed results. Failed saves stay
visible with a retry action; normal close awaits a successful save.
New saves use schema version 5. Versions 1 through 4 still load; old manual discards
do not retroactively earn CP. Their status tells the user CP was entered manually.
Undoing an old discard cannot infer or remove a manually entered CP gain.
Battle history is accessible during play after saving. Delete battle asks for
confirmation and removes only that battle's JSON/temporary file. The store
serializes deletion with saves and rejects stale saves for a deleted battle.
Deleting the final entry leaves an empty history with a New battle button.

Remaining workflow work
This is a scoring UI, not a fully automated mission/deck referee. Tactical cards
can be selected from a physical deck or drawn randomly. Ordinary draws, carried unscored cards,
achieved cards, discards and paid/free discard replacements are recorded.
Players choose Shuffle Back when the card rule allows it; optional battlefield
conditions are not checked automatically. The random picker handles Defend Stronghold's mandatory
round-one shuffle-back by excluding it until a later round. Target/unit nominations and action
progress must currently be tracked on the table. CP is entered manually; automatic Command phase grants and army
exceptions are not implemented. Post-start disposition/mode/turn-order correction
requires a future explicit correction flow. Army rules are accessible by saving
and closing the tracker, then resuming it. Compare is future work; invitation/response exchange is implemented with files
and copied text. QR scanning remains unfinished.

Validation
- BattleTrackerChecks: catalogue completeness, order/snapshot/code, counts,
  OR/bonus semantics, timing/round restrictions, cross-turn round caps, 45/20 VP
  limits, correction propagation, Tactical lifecycle, CP and JSON persistence.
- BattleDiscardChecks (saved-event compatibility): shared reward cap, undo and later spending, redraw
  affordability, once-per-battle limit, replacement chains, achieved/Fixed card
  protection, free-discard eligibility, round trip and legacy save compatibility.
- Existing disposition/primary-card regression checks remain applicable.
- BattleActionChecks: normal/replacement wrong-card corrections, protected
  selections, random pools/draw limits, exhausted decks, early finish/reopen,
  current-schema persistence, scoped deletion and stale-save protection.
- BattleExchangeChecks: two-device identity/order/primaries, readable immutable
  rosters, bounded decoding, mismatched/changed invitations, saved drafts,
  repeated imports, separate score state and collision protection.
- BattleScoreboardChecks: permanent discards, shuffle/redraw, recovery/history/CP,
  compact per-card totals, carried cards and persistence.
- Native Windows smoke exercises setup, real scoring buttons, live totals/cap
  label, simple discard without CP changes, manual CP counters, achieved-card protection,
  round navigation, narrow-window layout, scoreboard and persisted result;
  random draw, simple discard, early finish/reopen, history finish,
  cancelled/confirmed deletion and the empty history state.
- Windows build and Android Compile target checked. Android physical gestures,
  packaged trimming and process-death lifecycle still need device/release checks.
- Android 0.2.16 (code 17) was packaged and installed on the connected phone
  on 2026-10-15. Cold launch restored the existing army. Both mission resources
  were included and native startup checks passed for both ABIs. Device security
  blocks injected taps, so tracker interaction testing remains with the user.
  See docs/distribution/android-0.2.16.txt. Windows installer remains unchanged.
